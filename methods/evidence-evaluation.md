# Evidence Evaluation Method

## Evidence Hierarchy

| Level | Type | Strength |
|-------|------|----------|
| 1 | Systematic reviews & meta-analyses — consistent findings across multiple studies | Highest |
| 2 | Randomized controlled trials — random assignment, confounders controlled | High |
| 3 | Observational studies — cohort and case-control; confounding possible | Medium |
| 4 | Case reports & expert opinion — no control group | Low |
| 5 | Anecdotal evidence — personal experience and testimonials; not generalizable | Lowest |

## Evaluation Dimensions

### 1. Source Credibility
- **Peer review** — has it undergone academic review?
- **Institutional reputation** — professional standing of the source?
- **Conflicts of interest** — financial or ideological stakes of the authors?
- **Citation record** — how is it cited by other research?

### 2. Methodological Rigor
- **Sample size** — sufficient to detect the claimed effect?
- **Sampling method** — random? representative?
- **Measurement tools** — valid and reliable?
- **Control variables** — confounding factors controlled?
- **Statistical methods** — appropriate? effect sizes reported?

### 3. Result Interpretation
- **Correlation vs. causation** — properly distinguished?
- **Statistical vs. practical significance** — is the effect size meaningful?
- **External validity** — do the results generalize to other contexts?
- **Selective reporting** — are all results reported, including negative ones?

### 4. Consistency & Replicability
- **Consistency** — do other studies find similar results?
- **Direct replication** — has the finding been replicated?
- **Conceptual replication** — do different methods reach similar conclusions?

## Red Flags

**Methodological**:
- ❌ Sample too small (e.g. n < 30 with strong effect claims)
- ❌ No control group
- ❌ Selective reporting of results
- ❌ p-hacking or data dredging
- ❌ Study design not pre-registered

**Interpretive**:
- ❌ Overclaiming causality
- ❌ Ignoring contrary evidence
- ❌ Exaggerated effect sizes
- ❌ Overgeneralized external validity

**Source-related**:
- ❌ Predatory journals
- ❌ Not peer-reviewed
- ❌ Undisclosed conflicts of interest
- ❌ Authors lack relevant expertise

## Evaluation Checklist

```markdown
### Source Assessment
- [ ] Is the source peer-reviewed?
- [ ] Journal/publisher reputation?
- [ ] Do the authors have relevant expertise?
- [ ] Any conflicts of interest?

### Methodological Assessment
- [ ] Sample size sufficient?
- [ ] Sampling method appropriate?
- [ ] Control group present?
- [ ] Measurement tools valid?
- [ ] Statistical methods appropriate?

### Results Assessment
- [ ] Effect sizes reported?
- [ ] Correlation vs. causation distinguished?
- [ ] Negative results reported?
- [ ] Conclusions supported by the data?
```

## Output Template

```markdown
## Evidence Evaluation Report

### Evidence Description
- Source: [full citation]
- Study type: [RCT / observational / case report / etc.]
- Main findings: [brief description]

### Quality Assessment
- Methodological quality: [high/medium/low]
- Evidence strength: [strong/medium/weak]
- Major limitations: [list 2–3]

### Applicability Assessment
- External validity: [high/medium/low]
- Relevance to the current question: [high/medium/low]

### Usage Recommendations
- Can serve as: [primary / secondary / reference support]
- Caveats: [warnings for use]
- Needs supplementation: [what additional evidence is needed]
```

Write the report in the user's language.
