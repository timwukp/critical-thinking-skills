# Bias Detection Method

Identify cognitive biases, framing effects, and systemic biases that distort reasoning and decision quality.

## Cognitive Bias Types

### 1. Confirmation Bias
Tendency to seek evidence supporting existing beliefs and ignore or dismiss contrary evidence.
**Detection**: Ask "What contrary evidence have you considered?"

### 2. Anchoring Effect
Over-reliance on the first information received; subsequent judgments stay "anchored" to the initial value.
**Detection**: Check for over-reliance on initial data or assumptions.

### 3. Availability Heuristic
Judging by easily recalled examples; overweighting vivid or recent events.
**Detection**: Ask "Is this example actually representative?"

### 4. Hindsight Bias
The "I knew it all along" effect — believing outcomes were predictable after the fact.
**Detection**: Check whether pre-event uncertainty is being ignored.

### 5. Framing Effect
The same information yields different judgments depending on how it is presented.
**Detection**: Restate the problem in a different frame and check whether the conclusion changes.

### 6. In-group Bias
Preference for one's own group's members and viewpoints; devaluing out-group contributions.
**Detection**: Ask "Would you accept this if an out-group proposed it?"

## Systemic Biases

### 1. Selective Citation
Citing only sources that support the conclusion, ignoring contrary research.
**Detection**: Actively search for contrary literature.

### 2. Survivorship Bias
Focusing only on successes, ignoring the lessons of failures.
**Detection**: Ask "What happened to the cases that failed?"

### 3. Publication Bias
Positive results are more likely to be published; the literature may overestimate effects.
**Detection**: Look for unpublished studies and pre-registered trials.

## Detection Techniques

### Technique 1: Consider the Opposite
Force consideration of the opposing view.
- "If the conclusion were the opposite, what evidence would support it?"
- "Under what circumstances would this judgment be wrong?"

### Technique 2: Outside View
Examine the reasoning as an external observer would.
- "How would a disinterested outsider evaluate this reasoning?"
- "What do the base rates / statistics show?"

### Technique 3: Pre-commitment
Record predictions before outcomes are known, to defeat hindsight bias.
**Application**: Create a traceable record of judgments.
