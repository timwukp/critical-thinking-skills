# Nietzschean Methodology for Deep-Critique Mode

Read this file only when deep-critique mode is active (the user explicitly asked for deep/philosophical critique, or the subject centrally involves values, morality, or social norms). These tools sharpen critique of *normative* claims; applied to technical or factual questions they produce noise.

Core stance: critique is not nihilism. Its purpose is to clear ground for better values and better arguments. Every Nietzschean critique must end with a constructive alternative.

---

## The Four Operational Tools

### 1. Genealogical Interrogation
**Source**: *On the Genealogy of Morals* (1887) — the method of tracing a concept's descent (*Herkunft*) and emergence (*Entstehung*) to expose its contingency and the power relations behind it.

Ask of any norm, concept, or "self-evident" premise:
- What is the historical origin of this idea? Was it always held, or did it emerge at a specific moment, for specific reasons?
- How did this premise come to feel "natural" or "obvious"? What made questioning it taboo?
- Who benefits from its acceptance? Whose interests does the norm serve, and whose does it suppress?

**Output**: a short genealogy — where the idea came from, what it displaced, and who gains from it now.

### 2. Perspective Analysis
**Source**: perspectivism, developed in *Beyond Good and Evil* (1886) and the late notebooks. (The famous line "there are no facts, only interpretations" comes from the *Nachlass* — the unpublished notebooks, later printed as *The Will to Power* §481 — not from *Beyond Good and Evil* itself.)

Ask of any argument:
- From which standpoint does this argument proceed? What does that standpoint make visible — and invisible?
- Whose truth is this "truth"? Which actors would describe the same situation entirely differently?
- Steel-man at least one rival perspective: state its strongest form before comparing.

**Output**: the argument's own perspective made explicit, plus at least one steel-manned alternative frame.

### 3. Value Interrogation & Revaluation
**Source**: the "revaluation of all values" (*Umwertung aller Werte*), the program of Nietzsche's late works — *Twilight of the Idols* (1889, "philosophizing with a hammer"), *The Antichrist*, *Ecce Homo*. Related: the master/slave morality distinction, developed in *Beyond Good and Evil* §260 and the First Essay of the *Genealogy*.

Ask of any argument:
- What values does this argument presuppose without defending (e.g. "growth is good", "efficiency over fairness")?
- Are those values serving the people invoking them, or inherited unexamined?
- What alternative value orderings exist, and how would the conclusion change under them?

**Output**: the hidden value judgments named, and the conclusion re-derived under at least one alternative value ordering.

### 4. Robustness Test (Eternal Recurrence, operationalized)
**Source**: the eternal recurrence thought experiment — *The Gay Science* §341, dramatized in *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* (1883–1885).

Nietzsche's test asks whether you could affirm a choice if it recurred forever. Operationalized for arguments:
- Does this argument hold across *all* plausible scenarios, or only under favorable conditions?
- Repeat the reasoning across time, scale, and adverse contexts — where does it first break?
- Would the argument's proponents still endorse it if they had to live under its conclusion indefinitely, in the worst case?

**Output**: the boundary conditions under which the argument fails, stated concretely.

---

## Affirmative Synthesis (mandatory closing step)

Destruction alone is incomplete. After the layers above:
1. State what survives the critique — which parts of the argument remain sound.
2. Propose at least one constructive alternative: a revised claim, a re-ordered value premise, or a stronger argument for a defensible version of the conclusion.
3. Keep the critique aimed at ideas, never at persons.

## Source Notes (for accurate citation)

| Concept | Primary source |
|---------|----------------|
| Genealogical critique; master/slave morality | *On the Genealogy of Morals* (1887); also *Beyond Good and Evil* §260 |
| Perspectivism | *Beyond Good and Evil* (1886); "no facts, only interpretations" is from the *Nachlass* (printed in *The Will to Power* §481) |
| Revaluation of all values | Late works: *Twilight of the Idols* (1889), *The Antichrist*, *Ecce Homo* |
| Eternal recurrence | *The Gay Science* §341; *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* (1883–1885) |
| Übermensch, "God is dead" | *Thus Spoke Zarathustra*; "God is dead" first in *The Gay Science* §108/§125 |

**Caution on *The Will to Power***: not a book Nietzsche wrote. It is a posthumous compilation of notebook fragments arranged by his sister Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche (with Peter Gast), whose editing is widely regarded as distorting. Cite it as *Nachlass* material, not as Nietzsche's considered position.
