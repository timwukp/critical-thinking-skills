# Reasoning Quick Checklist

A one-page sanity check for arguments, claims, and plans. Use when a full method file is overkill. Report results in the user's language.

## The Argument

- [ ] Can I state the conclusion in one sentence?
- [ ] Can I list the premises it rests on?
- [ ] What is the strongest *unstated* assumption? Would the conclusion survive its failure?
- [ ] Is the reasoning type clear (deductive / inductive / analogical), and does it meet that type's standard?
- [ ] Any obvious fallacy? (Check `fallacy-reference.md` if a specific one is suspected.)

## The Evidence

- [ ] What kind of evidence is offered (meta-analysis → RCT → observational → expert opinion → anecdote)?
- [ ] Is the sample big and representative enough for the claim's strength?
- [ ] Correlation being sold as causation?
- [ ] What contrary evidence exists, and was it addressed or ignored?
- [ ] Does the source have a stake in the conclusion?

## Red Flags (any one warrants a closer look)

- ⚠️ Conclusion far stronger than the evidence ("proves", "always", "never")
- ⚠️ Only success stories cited — where are the failures? (survivorship)
- ⚠️ Either/or framing with no third option (false dilemma)
- ⚠️ The claim's main support is emotional force or authority prestige
- ⚠️ Key terms undefined or shifting meaning mid-argument

## Alternatives & Robustness

- [ ] What is the best *rival* explanation for the same facts?
- [ ] What single counterexample would most damage the argument?
- [ ] Does the argument hold at the boundaries (extreme values, opposite user behavior, market reversal)?

## Verdict

- [ ] Overall strength: strong / medium / weak — and the one-sentence reason
- [ ] Key uncertainties stated explicitly
- [ ] One concrete suggestion to strengthen the argument or settle the question
